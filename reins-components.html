What counts here

Personal expenses

The long name: Personal expenses run through the business
Spend that benefits you and your family rather than the business. A new owner doesn’t inherit it, so it comes back into earnings.
Common examples
  • Vehicles: a spouse’s car on the fleet policy, family fuel cards, personal auto insurance
  • Travel and meals: a family trip coded as training, a hunting lease, season tickets
  • Memberships: country club, golf, gym
  • Toys: the boat, the RV, the side-by-side
  • Personal legal, kids’ tuition, personal cards paid by the company
  • Charity that’s personal. Sponsoring the little league team that feeds you leads is marketing, the church donation isn’t
If we missed something here, add it from the category: Something personal you know is in there that we didn’t catch.

Rules worth carrying into Figma

The decisions that took longest to reach, written down so they don’t get designed away.

  • Add adds the number on the row. No percentages on buttons, no hidden arithmetic.
  • Doing nothing means it doesn’t count. Declining is the default, so nothing counts down to zero.
  • Every row is the same height in every state, so the list never reflows as decisions land.
  • Amounts right-align to one column — that column is how an owner scans for what matters.
  • Outcome doubles as undo on the row. In the panel there is room, so both show.
  • Nothing offers a one-click way to claim everything. One deliberate act per vendor.
  • Green means added, amber a deduction, grey declined. Colour never decorates.
